\begin{alertblock}{HIGHLIGHT}
\centering
\textbf{Slow post-event transients reveal structural connectome topology beyond spatial geometry.}

\begin{block}{From an event to a network trajectory}
\justifying

Movement onset provides a precise event marker. We use event-locked MEG responses to follow how cortical activity redistributes after the perturbation, rather than comparing only static spatial patterns.

\vspace{1mm}
\figureplaceholder{15.5cm}{FIG. 1 --- event-locked cortical response\\[1mm]
\small redesigned cortical activation + representative transient trajectories}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Finite-relaxation transport on a graph}
\justifying

Standard graph diffusion assumes that flux responds instantaneously to a gradient. Instead, let edge fluxes relax over a finite timescale:
\begin{bigequation}
\dot{\mathbf{x}}+\operatorname{div}\mathbf{J}=0,
\qquad
\tau\dot{\mathbf{J}}+\mathbf{J}
=-\operatorname{grad}\mathbf{x}.
\end{bigequation}

Eliminating the edge flux gives the graph telegrapher equation
\begin{bigequation}
\tcbhighmath[colframe=blockshade,colback=blockshade,boxrule=0pt,arc=2mm]{
\tau\ddot{\mathbf{x}}+\dot{\mathbf{x}}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x}=0
}
\end{bigequation}

\textbf{The temporal bottleneck is placed in transport along pathways, not in delayed node states.}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Topology sets the dynamical regime}
\justifying

For a Laplacian mode $\mathbf{L}\mathbf{v}_k=\lambda_k\mathbf{v}_k$,
\[
\tau\ddot z_k+\dot z_k+\lambda_k z_k=0.
\]
Mode $k$ becomes underdamped when $4\tau\lambda_k>1$. The first nontrivial transition is therefore
\begin{bigequation}
\tau_c=\frac{1}{4\lambda_2}.
\end{bigequation}

\figureplaceholder{14.5cm}{FIG. 2 --- overdamped $\rightarrow$ underdamped\\[1mm]
\small redesigned monotone vs oscillatory network relaxation}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Topology or geometry?}
\justifying

Structural connectivity is strongly constrained by spatial embedding. We therefore compare the empirical structural connectome against two spatial graph controls under the \emph{same} dynamical model:
\[
\text{Structural}
\qquad
\text{EDR}
\qquad
\text{Geometric}.
\]

\figureplaceholder{22.0cm}{FIG. 6 --- network architectures and graph modes\\[1mm]
\small redesigned structural / EDR / geometric comparison}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{The structural connectome wins at low frequencies}
\justifying

Across the four motor conditions, the empirical structural connectome gives the best trajectory-level fits. Its advantage over spatial nulls is strongest in fast-delta and theta, smaller in alpha, and weak in beta and gamma.

\vspace{1mm}
\figureplaceholder{25.0cm}{FIG. 4/5 --- topology advantage across frequency\\[1mm]
\small compact poster version of fit quality / paired $\Delta R^2$}

\begin{center}
\textbf{Slow post-event components are the regime in which large-scale structural topology is most visible.}
\end{center}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Take-away}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Finite edge-flux relaxation turns graph diffusion into a hyperbolic transient dynamics.
    \item The Laplacian spectrum sets the overdamped--underdamped threshold through $\lambda_2$.
    \item In MEG data, structural topology explains low-frequency event-locked trajectories better than geometry-based graph nulls.
\end{itemize}
\end{block}
